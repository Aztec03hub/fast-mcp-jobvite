## THE POSITIVE CONTROL IS LOAD-BEARING AND THE PLAN SAYS WHY

> **An APPROVED write moves the row counter by exactly one, on both eras.** Without it, four refusal
> arms all asserting *the row count did not move* pass perfectly against a `create_candidate` that is
> broken and never writes at all - the guard-that-refuses-everything the blanket rule is named for.

**The whole harness rests on a server-side row counter**, exactly as the spike ran it. Without a
counter the refusal arms assert nothing - and `FASTMCP-SPIKE-4.md:1431` is the spike recording that
failure against itself. **Write the counter and the approved-write control FIRST**, the way U8 wrote
its positive control before any source file existed.

## Two traps that are measured, not theoretical

1. **NEITHER `ctx.transport` NOR `session_id` MAY BE THE DISCRIMINATOR.** Both are identical or
   populated on **both** eras - `FASTMCP-SPIKE-4.md:2073-2074` measured it. **Assert that the
   discriminator is `protocol_version`**, so a later refactor cannot quietly swap it.
2. **§8 #25 MUST NOT ASSERT AN ERROR SHAPE.** The no-handler arm **raises `MCPError` on sessionless
   and returns `is_error=True` on handshake** (`FASTMCP-SPIKE-4.md:2153-2165`). An error-shape
   assertion passes on one era and fails on the other. **Assert the ROW COUNT did not change.**

§8 #22 has four arms and the second is the one people drop: deny refuses; **accept carrying
`approve: false` REFUSES**; no-handler fails closed; and the second leg **actually consumes**
`ctx.input_responses`.
